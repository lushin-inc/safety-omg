SafetyNet

Steven Brooks — Sales Development Plan

Overview
The situation

SafetyNet is asking two things to change at the same time. The business is moving from consulting-led revenue to software-led revenue, and 28 years of selling is moving from the owner to the company's first salesperson. Both assessments say the same thing from different directions: the company has a reputation, a referral engine and a product it believes in, and it has almost none of the structure that would let a second person sell any of it. Sales capability across the two people assessed averages 34 against a target of 67, and the competencies furthest from target are precisely the four the software sale depends on — reaching a decision-maker, uncovering a compelling reason to change, discussing money, and getting a decision. The improvement available is real, at 45 percent, and both people assess as coachable at target. What is missing is not willingness. It is a defined process, a shared system, and a management rhythm that has never had to exist.

How to read every number in this plan

Each score is measured against a target for that competency, shown as the tick mark on the bar. Targets come from OMG's data on the level a competency has to reach before it stops limiting results — they are not averages, so scoring below one is common. Where both people were assessed on a competency, the figure shown is the average of the two.

What each one means for the goal

The lowest score in the whole assessment. Consultative selling is the ability to get a prospect to describe their own problem, in their own words, at a cost they can quantify. Both assessments instead show a pattern of educating and explaining.

This is the single biggest obstacle to selling software against an incumbent. A prospect who has been educated knows what the product does. A prospect who has been questioned properly knows what staying put is costing them. Only the second one switches.

Qualifying is knowing, before you invest time, whether there is a budget, a decision-maker, a decision process and a real reason to act. At 14 the company is largely finding that out by proceeding.

The clearest example is the five-year software conversation with the large defense customer. Interest has been established. Budget ownership, decision process and a decision date have not.

Both people scored within a point of each other here, which makes this a company pattern rather than a personal weakness. Both assessments flag the belief that a prospect who wants to think it over will eventually buy.

Software evaluations do not stall politely and then resume. They go quiet while the incumbent stays installed.

This average hides the widest split in the assessment: one of the two people scores 57 and is classed as a potential hunter, the other scores 0. All of the company's new business has come from referral, partnership and reputation, which has worked for 28 years.

It stops working when the goal is a specific revenue number by a specific date. Referrals arrive on their own schedule.

Both people scored exactly 29. This measures how they buy in their own lives — and both think purchases over, research them, and comparison shop before deciding.

Sellers who buy that way extend the same courtesy to their prospects without noticing. It is the quiet reason deals take years rather than quarters, and because it is identical across both people it will reproduce itself in the next person hired.

A 43-point split between the two people, which tells you there is no shared definition of how a SafetyNet deal is won. One person is following a process that exists only in their own head; the other is following her own read of it.

Everything else in this plan depends on fixing this first. You cannot hold someone accountable to a process that has never been written down, and you cannot coach a stage you have not defined.

Both people scored exactly 39. Selling value is the ability to make the cost of the current situation concrete enough that price becomes a secondary question.

For consulting, reputation has carried this. For software, where the objection is "we already have one" followed by "switching is too much work," reputation is not enough — the prospect has to be able to put a number on staying.

Another wide split. One person is a capable daily CRM user scoring 80; the other scores 0 and is not in a CRM at all. Social selling is near zero for both.

The consequence is that the company has no shared view of its own pipeline. The good news is that the capability already exists in the building — it simply is not where the pipeline is being managed.

The pattern worth noticing

Four of these eight are effectively identical across both people — Supportive Buy Cycle at 29 and 29, Selling Value at 39 and 39, Closing at 27 and 26, and overall tactical capability at 32 and 35. When two people who have never worked a sales process together score the same on the same competencies, that is not a coincidence about them. It is how the company sells, and it will show up in the next hire unless it is addressed at the level of process rather than personality.

1. Both people are coachable, and both are optimistic

Coachability comes in at 75, exactly on target, for both people independently. Outlook is 100 against a target of 75 for both. Those two together are the conditions under which development actually sticks — the willingness is present and neither person is discouraged about where the business is headed.

2. The company does not discount

Both assessments show a focus on value over price, a high tolerance for large numbers, and an unwillingness to put out an inappropriate quote. One of the two also shows a specific strength in protecting margins and in avoiding time spent on unqualified prospects.

Protect this deliberately

In an incumbent-displacement software sale the standing temptation is to discount your way in. SafetyNet has a 28-year pricing culture that does not do that, and it is shared rather than resting on one person. Nothing in this plan should train it out.

3. The two people are strong in opposite places

The widest gaps between them are not a problem to fix — they are a teaching resource. Where one is at 80, the other is at 0, and vice versa:

  • CRM discipline — 80 points apart: the rep at 80, the owner at 0
  • Working independently — 72 points apart: the rep at 88, the owner at 16
  • Motivation — 67 points apart: the rep at 70, the owner at 3
  • Doesn't Need Approval — 60 points apart: the owner at 60, the rep at 0
  • Hunting — 57 points apart: the rep at 57, the owner at 0

That means most of what has to be built can be modeled inside the company rather than taught from outside. The system side of selling — living in a CRM, working independently, keeping activity up without supervision — is already strong in one seat. The conviction side — commitment, willingness to be disliked in a sales conversation, drive for the result — is already strong in the other.

4. Prospecting will actually happen

One of the two assessments classes that person as a potential hunter, with strengths in picking up the phone, reaching the target prospect, and getting referrals from a network, alongside rejection tolerance above target. Prospecting is the activity most new salespeople quietly abandon. This one will not.

5. Video is already a strength, and the reputation is real

Video proficiency averages 78 against a target of 67, which matters for a California company selling nationally. And the reputation the business has built is not in dispute — it is what has produced referrals, the partner relationships, and a customer list that includes some of the largest organizations in the country.

The one management habit that already exists

Pipeline Management at 65 sits a single point off target. Underneath it: reviewing pipeline regularly, looking at the right metrics, and focusing attention on new and stalled business all register as strengths. Nobody taught that, and it is the foundation the rest of this plan is built on — it is the container everything else goes into.

Sales Management Posturing is also above target at 71, which means the confidence and composure the role requires are already there.

Where the coaching investment is needed

Coaching at 19, Motivating at 20, Accountability at 30 and Recruiting at 10 are the four unpracticed muscles. They are low because they have never been used, not because the capacity is absent — passion for coaching, handling joint sales calls, and giving recognition all show as existing strengths, and the team already reports respecting and trusting the owner.

The three specific things to develop, in order:
  • Debriefing instead of demonstrating. The current transfer mechanism is having the salesperson watch. That only passes on what she happens to notice, and it requires the owner in the room — which is the thing he is trying to stop. A written debrief after every joint call transfers more in twenty minutes than three observed meetings do, and it works when he is not there.
  • Not rescuing the deal. The assessment flags a reluctance to let a salesperson lose a piece of business. Paired with the plan of having her run meetings while he joins as the technical expert, the predictable result is that he takes the wheel when it gets uncomfortable, the revenue lands, and nothing transfers. This needs to be a written rule, not an intention.
  • Motivating to her wiring rather than his. The two motivational profiles are opposite — one extrinsic, one intrinsic — and the assessment shows recognition sitting at the top of one list and the bottom of the other. Sincere praise will land flat and be misread as indifference. What works is a target she sets herself and a scoreboard she owns.

The hiring gap is a present problem, not a future one

Recruiting at 10, with hiring criteria and interviewing both showing as weaknesses, means there is currently no method for judging whether the first salesperson is working out and no method for hiring the next one. The same capability is what the general manager hire will require. It is worth building once and using twice.

The plan
Action priorities

Six priorities in this sequence. The order carries more weight than the content: you cannot hold anyone accountable to a process that does not exist, you cannot manage a pipeline without a system, and you cannot coach a skill you have not defined. This works from the bottom of the triangle up — strategy, then structure, then staff, then skills.

0 of 6 underway
1
Strategy — first 30 days
Write down the two sales processes

Sales Process averages 32 against a target of 67, and the two people are 43 points apart — there is no shared definition to work from. The consulting sale and the software sale are also not the same sale: different buyer, different objection, different milestones.

One page each. Stages, and the milestone that has to be true before a deal advances. Make reaching the actual decision-maker an explicit milestone in both, because both assessments flag the belief that you have to work through a buyer or procurement contact first.

Done looks like: two written processes the salesperson can follow without asking what happens next.

2
Structure — first 60 days
One CRM, and pipeline review as a weekly rhythm

Sales Technology averages 40 against a target of 67, and CRM capability is 80 points apart between the two people. The skill already exists in the building; it is just not where the pipeline is managed. Give the build to the person who scores 80 and configure it to the two processes from priority one, so the stages in the system match the stages on paper.

Then a standing weekly pipeline review on the calendar — not on demand, not when something looks stuck. Pipeline Management at 65 is the strongest management competency in the assessment and it currently has nothing to work on.

3
Structure and staff — ongoing from day 30
Replace watching with debriefing

Coaching scores 19 against a target of 67. A fixed weekly coaching block, protected like a customer meeting. Joint calls where the salesperson runs the meeting and the owner is the technical resource — the role he already handles well according to his own assessment.

One rule, in writing: he does not take the wheel mid-meeting. If a meeting goes badly it goes badly, and it gets debriefed afterward. This is the rule that makes the rest of the plan possible.

Every joint call gets a written debrief — what she was trying to accomplish, what actually happened, what she would do differently. Twenty minutes.

4
Skills — from day 60, once one through three exist
Build qualifying, decision-maker access and the money conversation

Only now. This is where most companies want to start, and starting here is why most sales training does not stick. Four competencies, all of them load-bearing for the software sale:

  • Consultative Selling, averaging 12 — getting the prospect to describe the problem instead of being told about the product
  • Qualifying, averaging 14 — budget, decision-maker, decision process, reason to act, established before the time is spent
  • Comfortable Discussing Money, averaging 42 against a target of 66
  • Closing, averaging 26 — asking for a decision instead of accepting a think-it-over

Run it against a live account rather than in the abstract. The five-year software conversation with the large defense customer is the obvious candidate: interest is established, and budget ownership and a decision date are not. If this work moves that account, the work is landing.

5
Staff — 30, 60 and 90 days
Put checkpoints on the staffing bet, and build selection capability

Recruiting scores 10, with hiring criteria and interviewing both weak. The first salesperson was moved into the role rather than hired for it, which was the sensible move with the person available. It is still an untested bet and it deserves a checkpoint rather than a hope.

Agree now what her prospecting activity should look like at 30, 60 and 90 days — conversations held, meetings set, decision-makers reached — and review it against what actually happens. Behavior, not attitude. Coachability is at target, so if the activity is there the skills follow.

Build the selection capability alongside it. It is needed for the general manager hire regardless of how this one goes.

6
Strategy — quarter two
Turn the partner channel into something managed

Hunting averages 28 against a target of 67, and the company's own history says partnering is where most of the success has come from. Today that channel is passive — partners bring work when SafetyNet happens to come to mind.

Name the partners, decide what a referral ask actually sounds like, put a cadence on it, and set a number. Relationship Building at 44 is the strongest tactical area either person has, so this is the highest-yield use of existing capability rather than new capability.

It also answers the credibility problem a twelve-person company faces when selling software at this scale: a partner's endorsement removes the objection before the first meeting.

Thirty-three percent on supportive conditions is the honest part. It says progress here will need structure and outside pressure rather than self-propulsion. A realistic timeline

First 90 days — infrastructure only, no skills training:
  • Two written sales processes
  • One CRM configured to those processes
  • A standing weekly pipeline review
  • A standing weekly coaching and debrief block
  • Agreed activity numbers at 30, 60 and 90 days
Months 4 through 9 — skill work against live accounts:
  • Qualifying and decision-maker access
  • Quantifying the cost of staying with an incumbent
  • Money conversations and asking for decisions
  • The partner channel run as a managed pipeline
Months 10 through 18 — the handoff becomes real:
  • The salesperson running meetings with the owner as a technical resource, not a closer
  • A pipeline the owner can read without being inside the deals
  • A selling function that would keep running if he stepped away
What has to be true for this to work

Two behaviors are worth more to this plan than anything on the skills list. The weekly blocks survive the weeks when the trade show, the software and the consulting delivery all land at once. And when a deal is going sideways on a call the owner is sitting on, he leaves his hands off the wheel.
