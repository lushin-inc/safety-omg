The plan
Your first 60 days

What the first 60 days should actually contain.

Yours to own:
  • The CRM build. Steven's CRM score is 0 and yours is 80. Once the sales process is written down, configuring the system around it is your work, not his.
  • Your activity numbers. Conversations held, meetings set, decision-makers reached. Agree them with Steven up front rather than being measured against a number you did not set. Your report says you respond to your own pressure far better than to someone else's, so set them yourself.
  • Running the meetings. You take the meeting. Steven comes in on the technical side when the conversation needs it — not to take it over.
What to ask Steven for, specifically:
  • A written debrief after every joint call. What you were trying to do, what happened, what you would do differently. Twenty minutes. This is how 28 years of his knowledge actually gets to you — watching him sell transfers only what you happen to notice.
  • The sales process on paper. Both of them — consulting and software are different sales. You should not have to ask him what happens next.
  • Coaching that challenges you, not coaching that reassures you. Your assessment says you tend to ask for pricing help, proposal help and encouragement, and not for the kind of coaching that pushes you. Encouragement feels better and develops nothing. Ask for the harder version.
The one habit to build first

You have written goals and a plan, but no system for tracking progress — the one gap in an otherwise strong set of self-management habits. Put your activity numbers somewhere you look at daily. Everything else gets easier once you can see whether you did the work.
